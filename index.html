💡 What is sorting?

Sorting means putting things in order, like lining up from shortest to tallest.

A computer can only look at a few numbers at a time, so it needs a step-by-step plan. A step-by-step plan like this is called an algorithm. Here are 5 different ways to sort. Pick one to watch it work, step by step.

How do they compare?

"n" means how many numbers we are sorting. "About n × n steps" means: if you double the numbers, the work goes up about 4 times.

Algorithm The big idea in one line How much work? (for big lists) Fast when the list is already sorted?
Bubble Compare neighbors and swap if they are in the wrong order. Big numbers "bubble" to the end. About n × n steps (slow) Yes, if it stops early when no swaps happen
Selection Find the smallest number, put it first. Repeat with the rest. About n × n steps (slow) No, it always checks everything
Insertion Pick up the next number and slide it into the right spot, like sorting cards in your hand. About n × n steps (slow) Yes, very fast
Merge Split the list in half again and again, then merge the small sorted pieces back together. About n × log n steps (fast) No, but it is always fast
Quick Pick a "pivot" number. Put smaller numbers on its left and bigger on its right. Repeat on each side. Usually n × log n (fast), but n × n in the worst case No. With the last number as pivot, a sorted list is its worst case!
